심리·소비 — 잼 24종 앞에서 사람들은 왜 빈손으로 돌아섰나, 많을수록 좋다는 믿음을 흔든 '선택 과부하'와 그 재현 논란(논설)

For decades, retailers operated on the assumption that more choice is always better, stocking their shelves with dozens of identical-looking jams and shampoos in the hope of catering to every conceivable taste. Yet a now-famous experiment at an upscale grocery store complicated that belief: shoppers who encountered a tasting booth with twenty-four varieties of jam were far more likely to stop and sample, but strikingly less likely to buy anything, than those offered just six. Psychologists attribute this counterintuitive pattern to what they call choice overload, a state in which the effort of comparing countless alternatives becomes so taxing that people either postpone the decision or walk away altogether. Even when they do commit, an abundance of options tends to breed nagging regret, as every forgone alternative lingers as a reminder of what might have been. Subsequent studies, however, have struggled to replicate the effect, suggesting that whether a vast assortment paralyzes or empowers consumers hinges less on the sheer number of options than on how clearly they are organized and how well buyers know what they want.

구분taxable(과세 대상인) — 세금 쪽 뜻만 — taxing과 혼동 주의
physically taxing, mentally taxing

striking a. 눈에 띄는, 두드러진

There is a striking contrast between the two companies' growth rates.
두 회사의 성장률 사이에는 두드러진 대조가 있다.

💡 strike(치다)에서 — '눈을 탁 치는' 것이 striking. 단, 노사 맥락의 striking workers는 '파업 중인 노동자'라 뜻이 갈라진다.
파생strikingly(ad.) 눈에 띄게, 현저히strike(v./n.) 치다; 파업(하다)
a striking contrast, a striking resemblance, strikingly similar
