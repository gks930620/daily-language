경제 — 빅테크의 '킬러 인수'와 반독점 규제의 사각지대(해설 기사)

For decades, antitrust regulators judged corporate mergers largely by a single yardstick: whether the deal would push up prices for consumers. That standard, however, sits awkwardly with the digital economy, where many of the most dominant platforms offer their core services free of charge and make money instead by harvesting user data. Critics argue that tech behemoths have exploited this blind spot, snapping up promising startups before they could mature into genuine rivals — a practice some economists have dubbed 'killer acquisitions.' Because the targets are often tiny at the time of purchase, such deals tend to slip beneath the thresholds that would trigger a formal review, leaving watchdogs to discover the damage only in hindsight. In response, lawmakers on both sides of the Atlantic are weighing rules that would shift the burden of proof onto the acquirers, requiring them to demonstrate that a takeover will not entrench their dominance.
