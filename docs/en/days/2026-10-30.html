환경·산업 — 분리배출한 플라스틱은 정말 다시 태어날까? 생산된 플라스틱의 10%도 안 되는 재활용률과, 재활용이 '생산 감축' 요구를 돌리는 위안의 의식이 되었다는 비판(논설)

For decades, consumers have been reassured that the chasing-arrows symbol stamped on a plastic container means it will be reborn as something new, yet studies suggest that less than a tenth of all the plastic ever produced has actually been recycled. The problem is not merely one of public apathy: most plastics grow weaker and more brittle each time they are melted down, and the sheer variety of resins, dyes and additives makes sorting them prohibitively expensive. Virgin plastic, made cheaply from oil and gas, routinely undercuts recycled material on price, leaving recyclers with little incentive to process anything but the most valuable bottles. Critics argue that the industry promoted recycling not because it believed the system would work, but because doing so deflected attention from calls to curb production altogether. Until manufacturers are made to bear the cost of disposing of what they sell, they contend, the recycling bin will remain less a solution than a soothing ritual that lets shoppers keep buying with a clear conscience.
