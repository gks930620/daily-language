노동·경제 — 플랫폼 노동자의 고용 신분 분류를 둘러싼 공방(기사)

For the better part of a decade, ride-hailing and delivery platforms have insisted that the people who work through their apps are independent contractors, not employees — a distinction that spares the companies the cost of paid leave, severance, and payroll taxes. Labor advocates counter that the arrangement amounts to misclassification on an industrial scale, since the algorithms that assign jobs, rate performance, and deactivate accounts exert precisely the sort of control that has long defined an employment relationship. Courts in several jurisdictions have begun to side with them, presuming that a worker is an employee unless the hiring firm can rebut that presumption. The platforms retort that reclassifying their fleets wholesale would compel them to slash the number of available shifts, eliminating the very flexibility that attracts workers in the first place. Unpersuaded by either camp, a growing number of policymakers are gravitating toward a middle path: portable benefits that follow the worker rather than the job, financed by a small levy on each transaction and administered independently of any single employer.

💡 ad-(~쪽으로) + voc(목소리) — 누군가를 위해 '목소리를 내 주는' 사람이다. 명사는 [애드버컷], 동사는 [애드버케이트]로 발음이 갈린다. 동사는 advocate + 명사/동명사가 정석이고 advocate to do는 어색하다.

💡 ostend(내보이다) 계열로 '보여 주기용'이라는 말이다. 뒤에 but으로 진짜 이유가 따라오는 것이 거의 정해진 패턴이라, 이 단어가 보이면 반전을 예상하면 된다. 실제로는 부사 ostensibly가 더 자주 쓰인다.
