오늘의 문단

보건·산업 — 의약품 특허와 약가를 둘러싼 논쟁, 그리고 강제실시권의 한계(논설)

For decades the pharmaceutical industry has defended patent exclusivity as the price society pays for innovation: without a temporary monopoly, it argues, few firms would shoulder the outlays that bringing a drug to market entails. Critics counter that the system has drifted from that rationale, pointing to secondary patents stacked on trivial reformulations so that a profitable drug can fend off generic competition long after its original protection lapses. The consequences fall unevenly: where insurers and intermediaries do little to cushion the markup, a regimen costing a few dollars to manufacture may be priced beyond the reach of the very patients whose ailments it was meant to treat. Faced with such disparities, several governments have invoked compulsory licensing, which lets a state authorize generic production in exchange for a modest royalty, only to be met with threats of litigation and trade retaliation. Whether those threats are credible matters less than their chilling effect; so long as health ministries fear years of legal attrition, a provision meant to serve as a safeguard will remain a bargaining chip rather than a remedy.
