경제·소비 — 실험실에서 키운 다이아몬드가 무너뜨린 '희소성 신화', 보석의 가치는 돌인가 이야기인가(논설)

For more than a century, the diamond industry sold not a stone but a story: that a gem forged deep underground over billions of years was a rarity worth two months' salary, and that "a diamond is forever." That carefully contrived mystique is now unraveling, as laboratories can grow stones that are chemically identical to mined ones for a fraction of the cost. Since even trained jewelers cannot tell the two apart without specialized equipment, prices for lab-grown diamonds have plunged, and many younger buyers now spurn the mined variety as an overpriced relic of a clever marketing ploy. Yet the shift poses an awkward question for consumers: if any stone can be produced on demand, what exactly is the cachet of owning one, and why would anyone expect it to hold its value on the resale market? Some analysts argue that diamonds were never a sound investment to begin with, since the moment a ring leaves the store it depreciates sharply, a reality the industry's erstwhile slogan conveniently left out.
