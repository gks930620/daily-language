항공·기후 — 지속가능 항공연료(SAF)와 항공 탈탄소의 병목(논설)

Aviation accounts for a modest share of global emissions, yet long-haul flight has proved one of the most stubborn activities to decarbonize, since a jet crossing an ocean cannot be plugged into a wall. Battery and hydrogen propulsion remain decades from carrying passengers across the Pacific, so the industry's favored remedy is sustainable aviation fuel, a near-drop-in substitute for conventional kerosene, refined from used cooking oil, agricultural residue and similar feedstocks. The trouble is supply: such fuel still accounts for well under one percent of what airlines burn, costs several times as much as the fossil kind, and competes for feedstock with shipping and road transport. Regulators in Europe have therefore imposed blending mandates that ratchet upward each decade, on the premise that guaranteed demand will coax refineries into building capacity that no carrier would finance on its own. Skeptics counter that mandates without a matching push on feedstock supply will merely inflate fares and stall uptake, and that the industry's quieter problem — the warming caused by contrails rather than carbon dioxide — remains almost entirely unaddressed.
