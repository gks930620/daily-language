과학·환경 — 사라지는 밤하늘, 빛 공해의 생태·건강 비용과 LED의 역설(논설)

For most of human history, a clear night revealed thousands of stars, but today an estimated eight in ten people live beneath skies so saturated with artificial light that the Milky Way has become all but invisible. The glow is not merely an aesthetic loss: ecologists have documented how misdirected illumination disorients migratory birds, lures nesting sea turtles away from the ocean, and disrupts the nocturnal rhythms on which countless insects depend. Human health may suffer as well, since prolonged exposure to light at night suppresses melatonin, a hormone that regulates sleep, and has been tentatively linked to metabolic disorders such as diabetes. What makes the problem especially frustrating is that, unlike most forms of pollution, it could be reversed almost overnight, simply by shielding fixtures, dimming them during off-peak hours, and aiming them downward where the light is needed. Yet the widespread switch to cheap, energy-efficient LEDs has, paradoxically, made matters worse, as municipalities that saved money on electricity have often responded by installing brighter and more numerous lamps, a textbook case of what economists call the rebound effect.
