과학 — 숲속 균류 네트워크('우드 와이드 웹')를 둘러싼 대중 서사와 과학적 증거의 간극(과학 칼럼)

Beneath the forest floor lies a sprawling web of fungal threads that ecologists have nicknamed the 'wood wide web,' a network through which trees appear to swap carbon, water and chemical signals. Popular books and documentaries have eagerly seized on the idea, portraying wise 'mother trees' that deliberately nourish their own seedlings and warn their neighbors of looming insect attacks. Yet a growing number of researchers caution that the evidence for such altruistic behavior is far thinner than the public narrative suggests. Many of the much-cited field studies, they argue, were too small to rule out simpler explanations, such as nutrients merely leaking through the soil rather than being actively channeled along fungal links. None of this means the networks are imaginary; rather, it is a reminder that a captivating metaphor can easily outrun the data meant to support it.
