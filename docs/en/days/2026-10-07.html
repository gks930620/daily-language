과학·보건 — 가짜 약이 진짜로 듣는다? '위약(플라세보) 효과'가 의료에 던지는 질문(논설)

For decades, the placebo effect was treated as little more than a nuisance in clinical trials—a statistical noise that researchers had to subtract before they could judge whether a new drug actually worked. Yet a growing body of research suggests that the mere expectation of relief can trigger measurable physiological changes, from the release of the brain's own painkillers to a reduction in the stress hormones that amplify discomfort. Perhaps most counterintuitively, some studies have found that patients who were told openly that they were taking sugar pills still reported their symptoms easing, as if the ritual of treatment mattered as much as its contents. Such findings have left clinicians in an ethically fraught position: deceiving patients is plainly unacceptable, but dismissing a benign intervention that measurably attenuates suffering seems equally hard to justify. What the placebo ultimately reveals, critics and enthusiasts alike agree, is not that the mind can cure any illness, but that the context in which care is administered—trust, attention, and time—may be a treatment in its own right.

구분reluctant(꺼리는) — reluctant는 행동을 꺼림 전반, reticent는 '말하기'를 꺼림에 한정
reticent about, remain reticent

prerogative n. (고유한) 특권, 권한

Setting interest rates is the prerogative of the central bank.
금리 결정은 중앙은행의 고유 권한이다.

💡 pre-(먼저) + rogare(묻다) — 고대 로마에서 '먼저 의견을 물어볼 자격'. 그래서 아무나 아닌 '그 사람(기관)에게만 있는 권한'.
구분privilege(특권, 특혜) — privilege는 남보다 누리는 혜택, prerogative는 직위에 따른 결정 권한에 무게
royal prerogative, it's your prerogative, management prerogative
