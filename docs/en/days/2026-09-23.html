소비·경제 — 가격은 그대로 두고 양을 줄이는 '슈링크플레이션'과 단위가격 표시 규제의 한계(논설)

When the price of a chocolate bar stays the same but the bar itself quietly shrinks by a tenth, most shoppers never notice, and that inattention is precisely what manufacturers are counting on. Economists call the practice "shrinkflation," and although it is perfectly legal, critics argue that it amounts to a disguised price increase that erodes consumer trust far more than an honest markup would. Companies counter that soaring costs for raw materials, packaging and transport leave them little choice, and that shoppers, faced with a visible price hike, simply defect to cheaper rivals. Regulators in several countries have responded not by banning the tactic outright but by requiring retailers to display the unit price, the cost per gram or litre, prominently on shelf labels. Yet such labels work only if people actually read them, and research suggests that busy shoppers, scanning dozens of products in a matter of minutes, rely on familiar packaging rather than the fine print.
