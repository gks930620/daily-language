과학·연구윤리 — 유명 연구가 재현되지 않는 '재현성 위기'와 사전 등록 개혁을 둘러싼 논쟁(논설)

For decades, a finding published in a prestigious journal was treated as settled fact, but large-scale replication projects have revealed that many landmark studies in psychology and medicine cannot be reproduced. The problem is rarely outright fraud; more often, it stems from subtle biases in how research is rewarded, such as testing dozens of hypotheses and reporting only the one that reaches statistical significance. Because journals favor eye-catching results over careful null findings, researchers under pressure to publish are tempted to inflate their evidence, while studies that fail to confirm a sensational claim languish unpublished. Reformers argue that scientists should preregister their methods before collecting any data, so that a flawed analysis cannot be tweaked after the fact to produce a more attractive outcome. Critics counter that such rules may stifle exploratory work, but few dispute that science, whose authority rests on being self-correcting, must rectify its errors before public trust becomes as elusive as the robust findings it promises.
