보건·윤리 — 장기 기증을 '거부 의사 표시(옵트아웃)' 방식으로 바꾸는 개혁을 둘러싼 논쟁(논설)

Every year thousands of patients die waiting for an organ that never arrives, and the gap between the donor registry and the waiting list shows no sign of closing. Frustrated by chronic shortages, a growing number of countries have adopted presumed consent, under which every adult is treated as a willing donor unless he or she has formally opted out. Proponents argue that the change merely corrects for inertia: most people approve of donation in principle, yet few ever register, and the bereaved, confronted with the question at the worst possible moment, tend to refuse. Critics counter that consent which is merely assumed is tantamount to no consent at all, and that a state helping itself to the bodies of citizens who failed to fill in a form has quietly redrawn an old line. The evidence is fraught with caveats: donation rates have risen only where the switch was accompanied by investment in transplant coordinators and intensive-care beds, which suggests that the law alone does not close the gap.
