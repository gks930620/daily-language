경제·심리 — 이미 쓴 돈이 아까워 망해 가는 일을 멈추지 못하는 '매몰비용의 오류', 끈기는 언제 독이 되는가(논설)

Economists have long warned against the sunk cost fallacy, the tendency to keep pouring resources into a failing venture simply because so much has already been invested in it. Money that has been spent and cannot be recovered, they argue, should be irrelevant to any forward-looking decision, yet people routinely cling to doomed projects, from half-finished bridges to unwatchable films they paid to see. Psychologists attribute this stubbornness partly to loss aversion: abandoning a project forces us to acknowledge that the earlier outlay was wasted, an admission that stings more than continued spending does. Governments are hardly immune, for officials who championed a costly program often find it politically safer to extend its funding than to concede a mistake in public. Ironically, the very perseverance we praise in individuals may, when stripped of honest reassessment, become the trait that turns a modest loss into a ruinous one.

write off phr. v. (손실·부채를) 장부에서 털다, 손실 처리하다; 실패로 단정하다
