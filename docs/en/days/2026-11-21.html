오늘의 문단

환경·경제 — 눈이 오지 않는 스키장, 인공 눈에 돈을 쏟아붓는 리조트와 '사계절 산악 관광'으로 살길을 찾는 산골 마을(해설 기사)

For decades, ski resorts in the Alps and the Rockies treated a reliable winter as a given, building entire local economies around a season that rarely failed to arrive. That assumption is now melting along with the snowpack: unseasonably warm spells and erratic precipitation have shortened seasons at lower elevations, where rain increasingly falls on slopes that once lay buried under powder. Many operators have responded by investing heavily in snowmaking, yet artificial snow requires both freezing temperatures and vast quantities of water and electricity, making it a costly and, critics argue, ultimately futile way to stave off the inevitable. Some resorts, rather than hemorrhaging money on machines that cannot outlast the warming trend, are diversifying into hiking, mountain biking and summer festivals, hoping to reduce their dependence on a few frozen months. Whether such efforts can sustain towns for which skiing has long been the economic mainstay remains uncertain, but few observers expect the snow of their childhood to return in the foreseeable future.

  1. For decades, ski resorts in the Alps and the Rockies treated a reliable winter as a given, building entire local economies around a season that rarely failed to arrive.
