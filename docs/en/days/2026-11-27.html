경제·소비 — 블랙프라이데이의 '역대급 할인'은 진짜일까? 부풀린 '정가'로 할인 폭을 커 보이게 하는 기준가 표시 수법과, 쇼핑 광풍 뒤에 밀려오는 반품의 숨은 비용(해설 기사)

As shoppers flood stores and websites for Black Friday, consumer watchdogs are warning that many of the season's eye-catching discounts are less generous than they appear. A common tactic, known as reference pricing, involves displaying an inflated 'original' price next to the sale price, so that an ordinary markdown masquerades as a once-a-year bargain. Investigators who tracked thousands of items over several months found that a sizable share had never actually been sold at the higher price, or had been briefly hiked just weeks before the event to make the eventual cut look steeper. Retailers counter that prices fluctuate constantly and that savvy shoppers can easily compare offers online, yet critics argue that countdown clocks and 'only three left' banners are designed precisely to discourage such comparison. Meanwhile, the buying frenzy carries a hidden cost: a wave of returns in the following weeks, many of which end up not back on shelves but in liquidation bins or landfills.
