언어·교육 — 소수 언어의 소멸과 몰입 교육을 둘러싼 논쟁(논설)

Of the roughly seven thousand languages spoken today, linguists estimate that nearly half will fall silent within a century, as the last fluent elders die and their grandchildren grow up speaking only a dominant national tongue. The retreat is rarely voluntary: for generations, schools punished children for using their vernacular, and the resulting stigma persuaded parents that assimilation was the price of opportunity. Once intergenerational transmission breaks down, a language becomes dormant with startling speed, surviving in recordings and dictionaries but no longer in anyone's daily life. Communities that have reversed the decline tend to share one feature — immersion schooling, in which children are taught every subject in the heritage language rather than being given a cursory hour of it each week. Critics call such programs an expensive indulgence that hampers literacy in the national language, yet the evidence points the other way: bilingual pupils in well-run immersion schools generally match or outperform their peers, and the communities that run them report measurable gains in wellbeing.
