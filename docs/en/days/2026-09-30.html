오늘의 문단

사회·노동 — 퇴근 후 업무 연락을 끊을 권리('연결되지 않을 권리')와 그 법의 진짜 힘(논설)

When France introduced its so-called right to disconnect in 2017, many observers ridiculed the idea of legislating something as mundane as whether employees should answer emails after dinner. Yet the premise behind the law was hardly trivial: smartphones had quietly blurred the boundary between work and home, turning evenings and weekends into an unpaid extension of the working day. Studies have since linked this constant availability to burnout, sleep disruption and a corrosive sense that one is never truly off duty, even when no message actually arrives. Critics counter that rigid bans are ill-suited to global teams spread across time zones, and that many workers genuinely value the flexibility to log off early and catch up later at night. Perhaps the more telling lesson is that the law's real force lies not in penalties, which are rarely imposed, but in obliging employers to negotiate explicit norms rather than leaving them to unspoken expectations.
