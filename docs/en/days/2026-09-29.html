오늘의 문단

사회·보건 — 공중보건 문제가 된 '외로움'과 사회적 처방, 그리고 사라진 모임의 장소(논설)

Once regarded as a private misfortune rather than a public concern, loneliness is now being described by health officials as an epidemic whose toll rivals that of smoking. A growing body of research suggests that chronic social isolation is not merely unpleasant but genuinely detrimental, raising the risk of heart disease, stroke and dementia, and possibly shortening life itself. Alarmed by such findings, several governments have appointed ministers for loneliness, and some doctors now practice "social prescribing," referring isolated patients not to a pharmacy but to choirs, walking groups and community gardens. Critics, however, contend that these schemes treat the symptoms of a far more insidious problem: the gradual fraying of the institutions—unions, churches, local clubs—that once bound neighbors together without anyone having to plan it. If that diagnosis is right, no prescription, however well intended, can stave off loneliness unless policymakers are also willing to rebuild the everyday places where strangers become acquaintances and, eventually, friends.
