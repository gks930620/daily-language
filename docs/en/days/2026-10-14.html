사회·관광 — 관광객을 반기던 도시들은 왜 입장료를 받기 시작했나, '과잉관광(오버투어리즘)'의 딜레마(논설)

For decades, cities such as Venice and Barcelona courted visitors with open arms, treating every additional cruise ship and budget flight as a boon to local coffers. Lately, however, the welcome has soured: residents complain that their neighborhoods have been hollowed out by short-term rentals, that rents have spiraled beyond their reach, and that quaint squares, now thronged with selfie-takers, feel more like theme parks than living communities. In response, a growing number of destinations have begun to impose entry fees on day-trippers, cap the number of cruise passengers allowed ashore, and restrict new holiday lets in the most crowded districts. Critics contend that such measures amount to little more than a toll on the less affluent, since wealthy travelers will hardly be deterred by a few extra euros, while families on a tight budget may be priced out altogether. The deeper dilemma, perhaps, is that tourism is both the lifeblood and the bane of these places: curtail it too sharply and the local economy falters; leave it unchecked and the very charm that draws visitors may vanish for good.
